Appendix D — Glossary

Terms in the book, by chapter.

The architecture of a research article

IMRaD

Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion: the standard structure of research articles. → Chapter 1

Rhetorical move

A unit of text with a specific communicative purpose, such as showing a gap. → Chapter 1

CARS model

Swales’s Create a Research Space model of introductions: territory, niche, occupation. → Chapter 1

Research gap

What is not yet known or resolved, which the study addresses. → Chapter 1

Structured abstract

An abstract with labelled parts: background, methods, results and conclusions. → Chapter 1

Context-content-conclusion

Scheme for papers, sections and paragraphs recommended by Mensh and Kording (2017). → Chapter 1

Clear sentences

Readability

How easy a text is to read, often estimated with formulas such as Flesch Reading Ease. → Chapter 2

Topic position

The beginning of a sentence, which should hold old, linking information. → Chapter 2

Stress position

The end of a sentence, which should hold new, important information. → Chapter 2

Nominalisation

A verb or adjective turned into a noun (analyse → analysis), often making sentences heavier. → Chapter 2

Passive voice

Construction in which the subject receives the action (samples were stored). → Chapter 2

Plain language

Clear, direct writing that the intended reader can understand on first reading. → Chapter 2

Hedging and stance

Hedge

Word or phrase that softens a claim (may, suggest, possibly). → Chapter 3

Booster

Word or phrase that strengthens a claim (clearly, demonstrate). → Chapter 3

Stance

The writer’s attitude towards claims and sources. → Chapter 3

Overclaiming

Stating more than the evidence supports, for example causal claims from correlations. → Chapter 3

Reporting verb

Verb used to report sources (show, suggest, claim), which conveys stance. → Chapter 3

Common errors

False friend

Word that looks similar in two languages but has a different meaning. → Chapter 4

Uncountable noun

Noun with no plural form (research, evidence, information). → Chapter 4

Academic Word List

Coxhead’s list of 570 word families frequent across academic disciplines. → Chapter 4

Collocation

Words that habitually go together (conduct a study, draw a conclusion). → Chapter 4

Calque

Literal translation of an expression from another language (make a research). → Chapter 4

Peer review and correspondence

Cover letter

Short letter to the editor that accompanies a submission. → Chapter 5

Response to reviewers

Document that answers each reviewer comment and explains the changes. → Chapter 5

Major and minor revision

Editorial decisions asking for substantial or small changes before acceptance. → Chapter 5

Politeness strategies

Linguistic choices that soften requests and criticism. → Chapter 5

Rebuttal

Reasoned disagreement with a reviewer’s comment, supported by evidence. → Chapter 5

Presenting and networking

Signposting

Phrases that tell the audience where they are in a talk. → Chapter 6

Word stress

The syllable pronounced with more force in a word (aNALysis). → Chapter 6

Elevator pitch

A 30-second summary of who you are and what you research. → Chapter 6

Clarification request

A phrase used to check you have understood a question. → Chapter 6

Linguistic inequality

The extra costs that the dominance of English imposes on non-native speakers in science. → Chapter 6
