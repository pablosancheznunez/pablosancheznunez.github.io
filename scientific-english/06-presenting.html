Required reading

Amano, T.; Ramírez-Castañeda, V.; Berdejo-Espinola, V.; Borokini, I.; Chowdhury, S.; Golivets, M. et al. (2023). The manifold costs of being a non-native English speaker in science. PLOS Biology, 21(7), e3002184.

1. Speaking at conferences as a non-native speaker

Amano et al. (2023) found that non-native English speakers reported spending more time preparing oral presentations and that many avoided attending or presenting at international conferences because of language. Knowing this is useful in two ways: preparation pays off, and the difficulty is shared by most of your audience. International audiences value clarity far more than a native accent.

2. Signposting

Signposting language tells the audience where they are in the talk. It is even more important in a second language, for you and for listeners.

Function Phrases
Opening Today I’m going to talk about… / The question I want to address is…
Outline First, I’ll… Then… Finally…
Moving on Let’s move on to… / That brings me to…
Pointing to a visual As you can see in this graph… / The key thing here is…
Emphasising What’s really important is… / I’d like to stress that…
Concluding To sum up… / The main take-home message is…
Inviting questions I’d be happy to take any questions.

3. Pronunciation that matters

Intelligibility depends more on word stress and sentence stress than on individual sounds. Common problems for Spanish speakers:

  • Word stress in academic words: anaLYsis, ANalyse, deVELop, DAta or DAHta, preSENT (verb) vs PREsent (noun), PHOtograph, phoTOgraphy.
  • Initial s + consonant: study, Spain, specific (not estudy).
  • Final consonants and -ed endings: analysed (/d/), tested (/ɪd/), worked (/t/).

Mark the stress in your script, record yourself and listen.

4. Questions and discussion

  • Ask for clarification: Sorry, could you repeat the question? / Do you mean…?
  • Buy time: That’s a good question. Let me think…
  • Admit limits: I’m not sure, but my guess would be… / That’s beyond the scope of this study, but…
  • Redirect politely: That’s an interesting point; perhaps we could discuss it after the session.

5. Networking

Prepare a 30-second elevator pitch: who you are, what you study and why it matters, in plain words. Ask questions about the other person’s work, follow up by email within a week, and remember that coffee breaks are where many collaborations start.
