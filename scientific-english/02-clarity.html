Required reading

Plavén-Sigray, P.; Matheson, G. J.; Schiffler, B. C.; Thompson, W. H. (2017). The readability of scientific texts is decreasing over time. eLife, 6, e27725.

1. Scientific texts are getting harder to read

Plavén-Sigray et al. (2017) analysed more than 700,000 abstracts from biomedical and life-science journals published between 1881 and 2015. Readability, measured with standard formulas, decreased steadily, partly because of more general scientific jargon (“robust”, “underlying”, “novel”) and longer sentences. Hard-to-read texts reduce the reach and the reproducibility of research: a method that cannot be understood cannot be repeated.

2. Readers’ expectations

Gopen and Swan’s classic advice on scientific writing can be summarised in a few principles:

  • Subject and verb close together and early. Readers wait for the verb; a long subject delays understanding.
  • Topic position (the beginning of a sentence) holds old information that links to what came before.
  • Stress position (the end of the sentence) holds the new, important information.
  • One main idea per sentence. Long sentences are not wrong; long sentences that hide the action are.

Hard: The determination of the extent to which open data mandates were associated with increases in data reuse across disciplines was the aim of this study.

Clear: We examined whether open data mandates increased data reuse across disciplines.

3. Three habits that cloud sentences

Habit Example Better
Nominalisation (verbs turned into nouns: -tion, -ment, -ance) We performed an analysis of the data. We analysed the data.
Unnecessary passive It was found by the authors that… We found that…
Wordy phrases due to the fact that; in order to; a large number of because; to; many

The passive is not forbidden: it is useful when the agent is unknown or irrelevant (Samples were stored at −80 °C). Many journals now encourage we for the authors’ actions.

Lab: readability checker

Lab · runs in your browser; your text does not leave it

Paste a paragraph. The lab estimates the Flesch Reading Ease score (higher = easier; most scientific abstracts score below 30), the average sentence length, and highlights nominalisations, passive constructions and wordy phrases.

area = window.Lab.area
viewof parrafo = area("Your text", {rows: 7, submit: "Analyse", value: "In order to perform an evaluation of the effectiveness of the intervention, an analysis of the data was conducted by the research team. It was found that there was a significant reduction in attrition due to the fact that supervision meetings were held more frequently. The implementation of the programme is recommended."})
{
  const t = parrafo || "";
  const frases = t.replace(/\s+/g, " ").split(/(?<=[.!?])\s+/).filter(f => f.trim());
  const palabras = t.toLowerCase().match(/[a-z']+/g) || [];
  const sil = w => { w = w.replace(/e$/, ""); const m = w.match(/[aeiouy]+/g); return Math.max(1, m ? m.length : 1); };
  const nP = palabras.length || 1, nF = Math.max(1, frases.length), nS = palabras.reduce((s, w) => s + sil(w), 0);
  const fre = 206.835 - 1.015 * (nP / nF) - 84.6 * (nS / nP);
  const esc = s => s.replace(/&/g, "&amp;").replace(/</g, "&lt;");
  const prolijas = {"in order to": "to", "due to the fact that": "because", "a large number of": "many", "in spite of the fact that": "although", "at this point in time": "now", "it is important to note that": "(delete)", "with regard to": "about", "a majority of": "most", "conduct an analysis of": "analyse", "perform an evaluation of": "evaluate"};
  let marcado = esc(t);
  marcado = marcado.replace(/\b(\w+(?:tion|ment|ance|ence|sis))\b/gi, '<mark title="nominalisation">$1</mark>');
  marcado = marcado.replace(/\b(was|were|is|are|been|be|being)\s+(\w+ed|found|held|made|done|shown|seen|given|taken)\b/gi, '<u title="passive">$1 $2</u>');
  const vistas = Object.keys(prolijas).filter(k => t.toLowerCase().includes(k));
  const nivel = fre >= 60 ? "plain English" : fre >= 30 ? "fairly difficult" : "very difficult (typical of scientific prose)";
  const d = html`<div role="status"></div>`;
  d.innerHTML = `<ul><li>Flesch Reading Ease: <strong>${fre.toFixed(0)}</strong> · ${nivel}</li><li>${nF} sentences · <strong>${(nP / nF).toFixed(1)}</strong> words per sentence</li></ul>
   <p style="line-height:1.9">${marcado}</p>
   <p class="small"><mark>highlighted</mark> = possible nominalisation · <u>underlined</u> = possible passive</p>
   ${vistas.length ? `<p class="lab-medio" style="font-weight:600">Wordy phrases: ${vistas.map(k => `«${k}» → «${prolijas[k]}»`).join("; ")}</p>` : '<p class="lab-bien" style="font-weight:600">No common wordy phrases found.</p>'}
   <p class="small">These are heuristics: not every -tion word or passive is a problem. Use them to look again, not as rules.</p>`;
  return d;
}

Worked example

NoteEditing a paragraph step by step

Original (52 words): “The implementation of an evaluation of the effectiveness of the mentoring programme was carried out by the research team, and it was found that there was a significant reduction in the level of anxiety of the participants due to the fact that they had access to regular meetings.”

  1. Find the real action: evaluate. Make it a verb with a subject: We evaluated…
  2. Replace wordy phrases: due to the fact that → because.
  3. Turn nominalisations into verbs: a reduction in the level of anxiety → anxiety fell.
  4. Put new information at the end: …because they met their mentors regularly.

Edited (20 words): “We evaluated the mentoring programme and found that participants’ anxiety fell significantly, probably because they met their mentors regularly.”

Graded exercises

Basic. Rewrite: “An increase in the number of citations was observed.”

“Citations increased.” or, with an agent, “We observed that citations increased.”

Advanced. Edit a paragraph of your own writing with the four steps of the case and compare the readability scores before and after in the lab.

Report the word count and the score before and after, and explain each change. Check that no technical precision was lost.

Assignment

ImportantChallenge for chapter 2 · Make it readable
  1. Take the abstract of your latest paper or draft and analyse it with the lab.
  2. Rewrite it, reducing nominalisations, unnecessary passives and wordy phrases, without changing the meaning or losing precision.
  3. Report the readability before and after and explain three changes.
